ISSUE 06 · BUSINESS & MINDSET · 3 MIN READ · BY DIYA DAS

We have an ego problem.

We are sometimes way too attached to our own ideas. We know there’s a better way to do something. We know we could try a different approach but somehow we still choose to end up doing things the way we’ve always done them. Not because they’re the best but because they’re familiar. And honestly, this happens a lot in business and content creation.

Imagine, someone running a water manufacturing business. Instead of making yet another video on "5 ways to start a business" ,why not talk about what it actually takes to run a business in a place where things aren't exactly favourable? The challenges of the ground realities, the decisions that didn't work, the things you learn while actually running the business. Wouldn't that be more interesting than repeating advice to people who have already heard a hundred times?

And the funny thing is most of us secretly know where we're exactly lacking. We know which things need to change, which decisions aren't working and which areas we could do better. But the moment someone points it out directly we get defensive. We dismiss their opinion, question their experience or hit them with "You don't need to teach me how to run my business."

Sometimes we forget that being in the market for years doesn't automatically mean we've figured everything out. Experience matters absolutely. But so does the willingness to listen, unlearn and adapt.Maybe we don't need more ideas. Maybe we need to stop recycling the same ones and start questioning why we're so afraid of doing things differently.

“Sometimes the biggest barrier to growth isn't a lack of knowledge. It's the ego that refuses to let that knowledge in.”
